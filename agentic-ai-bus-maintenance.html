Agentic AI & Predictive Maintenance within the Public Bus Sector — closing the gap between what manufacturers sell and what operators actually need, one depot at a time.

Dhanaya Aditiyo, Yasmine Allawi, Sofia Collard, Natalie Navarrete, Lovisa Skogsberg, Imane Tamim & Ruiling Xu
01 · Background

Stockholm's bus network is going electric under contracts that were written for diesel. Somewhere between the OEM's spec sheet and the depot floor, the plan stops matching reality.

"How can services in the public bus sector be improved to better align with the needs of the operators, thereby generating value for manufacturers?"
→

A sector under pressure

Ten-year tenders, strict punctuality KPIs, and an expensive shift to electric fleets are colliding with operational routines built for an older kind of bus.

→

The unseen needs of operators

What operators actually struggle with day to day rarely reaches the manufacturers designing the next fleet.

→

An interorganizational focus

The study sits deliberately between two organizations — Public Transport Operators and OEMs — rather than inside either one.

→

Grounded in the field

Semi-structured interviews, literature research, and benchmarking studies across both sides of the relationship.

04 · Empirical Findings

Before anything else: what does a bad day actually look like on the ground?

Technical Director

High Asset Cost

E-buses are expensive, so reserves are small — availability risk is concentrated.

"You can't have 15–20% reserve like back in the diesel days… now it's just a few percent because buses are so expensive."

Data Overload

"We don't really have a good way of handling data today… people need to understand what the data says, not only computers."

Skills Gap

"We need different competencies among maintenance technicians: more electricians with digital and on-board/IT network skills."

Traffic Control Lead

On-Route Breakdown

"Common vehicle faults: AC not working, door issues, engine problems… buses enter limp mode… undrivable in service."

Unclear Bus Readiness

"We have a digital map showing all buses, but it doesn't show if a bus is actually ready for service. We have to check multiple systems for faults."

Cryptic Error Messages

"Many are cryptic or misleading, some have barely changed in 20 years. Clear, actionable messages would help a lot."
05 · The Other Side

Manufacturers face their own version of the same problem.

Organizational & Governance

Every PTO runs its own system, so there is no common integration point to design toward.

Technical & Data Standardization

Heterogeneous systems mean inconsistent data quality, format, and coverage across fleets.

Underlying pressure

Total Cost of Ownership

Capability & Knowledge

A knowledge gap among operator technicians, driven by the shift to electrification.

Regulatory & Compliance

GDPR and the EU Data Act limit what raw and personal data can move between organizations.

06 · Analysis of Results

The diagnosis: an ecosystem-level coordination failure. The data is rich — it's just locked in separate rooms.

PTO Real-Time Data
→

←

Pillar 01

Smart Diagnostics

A shared knowledge base combining live operational data with OEM technical data to pinpoint fault root causes fast, using an AI-powered case library.

→ Shortens repair cycles, minimizes workshop time
Pillar 02

Predictive Maintenance

Analyzing fault signatures to catch failures before a bus breaks down mid-route — identifying high-risk vehicles before they enter service.

→ Reduces unplanned downtime, improves fleet availability
Pillar 03

Interactive Training Tool

On-demand, interactive troubleshooting built from real and synthetic fault cases, closing the EV skills gap for new and existing technicians.

→ Standardizes technician competence, speeds onboarding

The same two roles, one depot later.

Technical Director

High Asset Cost, addressed

Catches faults before they become in-service breakdowns → reduces unplanned downtime, makes thin reserves stretch further.

Data Overload, addressed

"This pattern predicts a failure within X days." Raw data becomes a maintenance insight, not another dashboard.

Skills Gap, addressed

Acts as a digital expert colleague: "Here's the likely root cause, based on similar previous cases" — plus short learning modules generated from real fault histories.

Traffic Control Lead

On-Route Breakdowns, addressed

Risk patterns are spotted early and trigger preventive repairs — fewer buses fail mid-service, fewer "this bus again" moments.

Bus Readiness, addressed

A single health score combining active fault codes, predictive risk, and open workshop orders.

Cryptic Errors, addressed

"Stop safely, call control. Likely door sensor issue; bus can be restarted after reset." Plain language, generated live.

09 · What Could Stop This — and What Makes It Possible

Every barrier has a matching enabler.

Barrier

Data governance & ownership ambiguity

Who owns the agentic AI's insights? Governance is genuinely uncertain.

Enabler

Strong leadership & governance

Executive sponsorship and clear decision frameworks, set early.

Barrier

Privacy, security & commercial risk

GDPR, cybersecurity concerns, and exposure of proprietary information.

Enabler

Policy & regulatory support

Alignment with safety, data, and compliance standards from day one.

Barrier

Data quality & technical integration

Fragmented processes, organisational silos, legacy tools and mismatched standards.

Enabler

Technology maturity & expertise

Robust platforms, backed by real specialist support.

Barrier

Workforce resistance & skills gap

New tools land on technicians who are already stretched thin.

Enabler

Engaged & skilled workforce

Training, adoption support, and genuine EV competence-building.

10 · Implementation Plan

Three stops, not one big leap.

Stop 1 — Preparation

Get the ground ready

Strategic Alignment

Define and align PTO–OEM objectives and problems.

Data Readiness

Map, clean, and consolidate data; draft data-sharing and legal agreements.

Process & Technical Review

Analyse current workflows and integration needs.

Project Setup

Establish team, roles, and governance.

Stop 2 — Pilot Project

Prove it at one depot

Deploy & Integrate

Launch a minimum viable AI agent at a selected pilot depot and use case.

Test & Learn

Collect performance data and user feedback, run safely alongside existing procedures.

Refine & Validate

Improve the agent and workflows based on what the pilot actually shows.

Stop 3 — Scaling & Integration

Take it system-wide

Expand & Integrate

Roll out to more depots and bus models; connect to broader enterprise IT.

Enable Adoption

Deliver training and change management; formalise governance and feedback loops.

Optimise & Sustain

Improve algorithms, demonstrate impact, and introduce training & simulation tools.

11 · Conclusion

The breakdowns were never really mechanical. They were organizational.

→

A necessity for a more efficient maintenance & training tool

Not a nice-to-have — the skills gap and reserve shortage make it urgent.

→

A trickle-down effect on many layers of the organization

Proactive maintenance eases stress for drivers, improves the passenger experience, and opens new revenue for OEMs.

→

Embracing AI with caution

  • Data sharing comes with real risk
  • Company integrity and legal regulation set the boundaries
→

Initial feedback from interviewees: positive

  • A costly project, but a necessary one
  • Valuable for transferring tacit knowledge
  • Reduces cost through spare-bus optimisation
